# Adler analytical-reading prompts

Run these as three separate passes against the complete book source.

## Pass 1 — What is the book about? (Rules 1–4)

```text
Apply the first stage of Mortimer Adler's analytical reading to this book. Give each rule its own section.

Rule 1 — Classify the book by kind and subject. Is it expository? Is it mainly theoretical or practical? Explain why. If it uses memoir, dialogue, letters, interviews, or speeches, distinguish its literary form from its argumentative function.
Rule 2 — State what the whole book is about in one short sentence.
Rule 3 — Outline the major parts in their order and relationship. Reconstruct the architecture of ideas; do not copy the table of contents.
Rule 4 — Identify the problems the author is trying to solve, including explicit and implicit questions.

Use chapter- or section-level evidence. Mark anything the book does not state explicitly. Do not invent page numbers.
```

## Pass 2 — What does the book say? (Rules 5–8)

```text
Apply the second stage of Mortimer Adler's analytical reading. Give each rule its own section.

Rule 5 — Identify the author's key terms and define their special meanings.
Rule 6 — Identify the most important propositions. Provide exact-sentence quotation candidates and their chapter or section. Do not claim they have been mechanically verified.
Rule 7 — Reconstruct the main arguments as linked premises and conclusions. Distinguish deduction, evidence, analogy, and personal experience.
Rule 8 — State which problems were solved, which remain unsolved, and which may be limited by time, method, or viewpoint.

Use chapter- or section-level evidence. Do not invent page numbers.
```

## Pass 3 — Is the book sound? (Rules 9–15)

```text
Apply the third stage of Mortimer Adler's analytical reading.

Rule 9 — State the limits of your understanding and qualification to criticize.
Rule 10 — State that the goal is understanding, not winning an argument.
Rule 11 — Separate evidence-supported knowledge, first-person testimony, aesthetic judgment, and value preference.

Mark rules 12–15 as PROVISIONAL FOR THE READER TO DECIDE. Label every candidate as TEXT-INTERNAL or REQUIRES EXTERNAL VERIFICATION.

Rule 12 — Where might the author be uninformed, and what external evidence is needed?
Rule 13 — Which factual claim may be wrong, and what authoritative source would verify it?
Rule 14 — Where might the reasoning be illogical or unresolved? If no defensible candidate exists, say so.
Rule 15 — Where is the analysis incomplete? Which important aspect or alternative remains untreated?

Finish with Adler's four questions: What is the book about? What is being said in detail and how? Is it true, in whole or in part? What of it?
```
